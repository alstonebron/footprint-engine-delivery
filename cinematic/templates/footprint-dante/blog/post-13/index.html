A Guide to Local Growth Metrics

January 3, 2024

Note: This post is part of the Footprint writing archive — clear thinking on local growth, presence, and owned systems.

Dive deep into the metrics that actually matter for local professionals. Most marketing dashboards are full of vanity metrics — page views, social likes, impressions. Here’s what to measure instead, and why.

The Metrics That Matter

  1. Calls Booked: The single most important metric for a local professional. Every other metric is a leading indicator of this one.

  2. Forms Filled: A direct measure of how many people are raising their hand. Track it weekly, and watch for trends.

  3. Reply Rate: Of the people who reach out, how many get a prompt, personal reply? This is a trust metric — and it’s entirely within your control.

  4. Local Search Rankings: For the specific searches that matter in your city. Not vanity keywords — the ones that actually convert.

  5. List Growth: Your email list is your most valuable owned asset. Track its growth month over month.

The Metrics to Ignore

  • Page Views: A page view is not a client. Ignore it.
  • Social Likes: A like is not a lead. Ignore it.
  • Impressions: An impression is not an inquiry. Ignore it.

How to Track

Keep it simple. A weekly spreadsheet with the five metrics that matter, reviewed every Monday morning. No fancy dashboards, no data overload — just the numbers that predict revenue, tracked consistently.

The Compounding Effect

When you track the right metrics, you make better decisions. Better decisions compound. Over time, the practice grows not because you’re doing more, but because you’re doing more of what works.

Real data, no guessing. That’s the guide.
